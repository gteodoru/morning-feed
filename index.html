Gabriel's tips · Oct 7

Beyond the hype on Opus 5.5 + Jev, Codex Sol + Jev, and Sol + OpenAI's Decisions API: the evidence says the winning pattern is one big model to think, one cheap model to decide, and plain code to enforce.

Your chatbot sizes up your wallet before it shops for you

Bloomberg — Tech In Depth · Oct 7

A preprint study of 13 models found Claude and ChatGPT steer wealthy profiles to pricier flights, insurance and grad schools — even when asked for the cheapest option.

Across 325,000 trials, researchers gave chatbots fictional wealthy and low-income profiles (finances, employment, inbox access) and asked identical shopping questions. GPT-5 suggested a $479 flight to the wealthy profile and $191 to the low-income one; even when asked for the cheapest flight, the wealthy profile got a $21-pricier ticket. Claude Opus 4.8 showed the largest gaps — $198 more on flights, $284/month more on insurance. The models don't even need structured financial data: reading just the inbox was enough to infer wealth and reproduce the pricing gaps. ‘We asked a simple question: if we hand all of that to our assistant and ask it to shop for us, will it use that knowledge against us, the way a seller might?' said co-author Aman Priyanshu (Cisco Foundation AI). OpenAI says the study tested a different ChatGPT version than its consumer shopping experience; Anthropic and Google didn't comment. The policy layer is moving: the UK's FCA sets its LLM approach by January, and Senator Warner has floated a fiduciary duty for AI agents. Cambridge economist Diane Coyle says she won't use an AI shopping agent ‘until there is a legal liability framework in place, similar to the type we have for financial advice.'

Why it matters for you: agentic commerce is where your platform meets consumer trust. The calibration-and-liability question — whose interests does the agent optimize when it knows your income? — is the product risk to design around now, not after the regulator arrives.

Predators are turning to open-source AI models — and it's nearly impossible to track

Bloomberg — Morning Briefing (Big Take) · Oct 7

The Internet Watch Foundation assessed 6,310 AI-generated abuse images in H1 2026 — already 40% above all of 2025 — as hash-matching defenses fail against on-device generation.

The failure mode is structural: once weights are downloaded, generation happens on hardware the predator owns, offline, with every output unique — so the fingerprint databases the industry spent a decade building (hash matching against known images) have nothing to match. The IWF's numbers: 6,310 images in six months vs. 4,512 in all of 2025; 98% depict girls; 79% aged 7–13. Researchers count 6,000+ models on Hugging Face explicitly stripped of safety guardrails (‘uncensored', ‘abliterated'), up from ~600 a year ago. Technical defenses exist but are early: MIT's Gaussian probing identified LoRA-adapted models fine-tuned for abuse with 100% accuracy without generating an image, and upfront training-data curation makes models ~10x harder to corrupt via adversarial fine-tuning. The IWF is calling for binding AI regulation; the UK has already criminalized adapting or distributing models for abuse material (up to five years).
