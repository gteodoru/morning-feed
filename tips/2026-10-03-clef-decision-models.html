Cloudflare's Clef: the open-weight decision model

Added Oct 3 · original article

A model that doesn't chat — it decides — and an infrastructure giant's bid to own the control layer of agentic AI.

What happened

On October 1, 2026, Cloudflare released Clef (27B) and Clef-flash (9B) — the first models trained in-house by its Workers AI team. A "decision model" takes an input state (text, images) plus a schema of typed questions — yes/no, pick-one choice, or score-against-a-rubric — and returns a probability for every allowed answer, with no free-text generation. The target: the hot path of agentic workflows — routing, triage, escalation — sitting between LLMs (open-ended, slow, non-deterministic) and classic classifiers (fast, but needing retraining for every new category). Both models are hosted on Workers AI and downloadable from Hugging Face under Apache 2.0.

How it works (architecture)

Backbone: frozen Qwen base models (Qwen3.8-27B for Clef, Qwen3.5-9B for flash), post-trained for decision use. Inference is a prefill-only pass — the decision step is non-autoregressive: instead of generating tokens, the model scores all valid schema choices in parallel through a "two-stage attention routing" scoring head. Training: rank-256 LoRA adapters, label-smoothed cross-entropy plus Brier loss for probability calibration, synthetic data, and an in-house method called RLCD (Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions).

Vendor numbers (read as claims): across 43 eval runs on the Jev Decision Index, a Clef model leads 7 of 10 headline benchmarks; median latency Clef 209ms / Clef-flash 38.8ms vs Jev 524ms. Versus Jev it adds a vision encoder (Jev is text-only), a 64k context window (vs 32k), and is fully Jev-API compatible — a drop-in swap. Internal dogfooding: Threat Intelligence classifying domains in 2.2s vs 4.7s for gpt-oss-120b.

The RL fine-tuning product

Alongside the models, Cloudflare launched an RL fine-tuning service: initially hands-on via a forward-deployed engineer team, later self-serve. The building blocks: AI Gateway (auto-captures a request dataset), Workers AI (rollouts against base Clef), Containers (RL sandbox for scoring/replaying agent actions), a new "Trainer" (weight updates), and BYO Model (redeploy onto Workers AI). Cited internal uses: Trust & Safety submissions, support triage, bot/crawler classification — leaning on Cloudflare's 15+ years of network data. No launch customers named; no pricing in the post (community-reported hosted pricing: Clef $0.24/M input tokens vs Jev's $0.042).

What people are saying

The HN thread (626 points, 217 comments) was awestruck and skeptical in equal measure. manlymuppet's top comment captured the disbelief at the speed of commoditization: Jev defined the category on Sept 15 and was beaten "based on Typesafe's own ranking" within weeks. buildbuildbuild's "Open weights, not open source" comment got the thread title edited by mod dang — the data and training pipeline aren't published. TeMPOraL called it "low-hanging fruit that's been lying around for years"; woah argued Jev's real moat was always the API and ergonomics, "very easy to copy an API"; janalsncm noted "the hard part is the data and evaluation."

The most important data point came from real user pdlug, who ran Clef vs Jev on a real task (should an agent's knowledge-base write go to human review?): quality was close (recall 0.98 vs 1.00) but hosted p50 latency was Clef ~850ms vs Jev ~110ms — directly contradicting the vendor latency story — and Clef-flash over-escalated (data + script at github.com/nicia-ai/admission-decision-eval). vulture916's pricing math: one million decisions costs ~$12.60 on Jev vs ~$72 on Clef hosted — "would probably make sense to self-host Clef." explainx.ai reported another HN user finding Clef 2–3x slower than Jev in their setup. Ground Truth was the strongest skeptic: "established classification practice repackaged," with no published calibration curves. On Reddit r/LocalLLaMA (354 pts, 107 comments), the top comment was "This is exactly what we needed in the local space," with the open question of how quality survives quantization below Q8. StartupFortune framed the week as a category pile-on: Amazon shipped open-weight Strands Decider 2B the same day, OpenAI has a closed Decisions API.

What to take away

Three takeaways. First, decision models are now a legitimate product category — a big infrastructure vendor treats fast, bounded, calibrated classification as a distinct layer of the agent stack, not a prompt-engineering trick. Second, the open-weight strategy flips the lock-in math: even if hosted Clef is pricier per decision than Jev, you can pull the weights down and own your decision layer forever. Third, the RL fine-tuning product reveals where Cloudflare thinks the moat is — not the base model, but the data flywheel: AI Gateway captures your traffic, Containers run your rollouts, and you get back a custom-tuned decision model.

For a platform builder, the directly actionable idea is the pattern itself: gate agent actions behind cheap, calibrated decision models instead of expensive LLM calls — whether or not you use Cloudflare's models. And the community's advice stands: benchmark p50/p95/dollars-per-1k-decisions on your own distribution; ignore vendor microbenches.
