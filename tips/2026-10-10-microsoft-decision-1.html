Nadella announces Microsoft-Decision-1: Microsoft enters the decision-model race

Added Oct 10 · original article

Microsoft's new model doesn't chat — it decides. At $0.042 per million tokens, Nadella is betting the future of agents is cheap, calibrated judgment calls, not expensive prose.

What the post says

On Friday, October 9, 2026, Satya Nadella announced Microsoft-Decision-1: a purpose-built AI model that scores fixed choices instead of generating text, aimed at routing, classification, verification, and agent workflow control. As quoted verbatim by multiple press outlets: "Introducing Microsoft-Decision-1, our new model for fast decision-making. It delivers top performance on structured decision tasks, outperforming both LLMs and other decision models in latency and quality. We're already testing it across Microsoft for everything from incident response and quality control to scientific discovery."

One caveat up front: the tweet itself couldn't be opened directly, so the quote above is reconstructed from multiple independent press outlets that quoted it verbatim — they agree with each other.

The model

Microsoft-Decision-1 is a decision-scoring model: given a situation and fixed options, it returns a calibrated probability per option in a single pass — yes/no, multiple choice, ratings, rubric-based grading of AI responses and agent actions — via a structured Decisions API. It does not generate open-ended text.

Under the hood it is built by post-training Alibaba's Qwen3.5-9B (~9B parameters, 32K context); Microsoft plans to rebase future versions on other foundations, including its own MAI models and OpenAI technology. It is live in Microsoft Foundry, with OpenRouter availability coming soon. Pricing: $0.042 per million input tokens, output tokens free (one Seeking Alpha repub prints "$0.42" — likely a typo).

Microsoft's claimed results: highest accuracy across 36 blind benchmarks covering ~150,000 questions; 4.5× faster than runner-up Quyet-1.0-Large and 35× faster than GPT-6 Sol at P50 latency; decision flips only 1.3% of the time under input perturbations; safety testing on 5,250 requests across 11 benchmarks (jailbreaks, prompt injection). Internal use cases cited: Xbox Research classified 10,000+ player reviews at GPT-6 Sol-competitive quality, 14× faster and 200× cheaper; Copilot QA competitive with GPT-5.6 Luna at 100× speed; Microsoft Discovery 46× more consistent than LLM scoring at 3× speed. The Foundry catalog warns Decision-1 "should not be the sole automated decision-maker for consequential matters involving employment, credit, housing, healthcare or legal rights."

What people are saying

Coverage is under 48 hours old and HN-centric so far. The HN thread (~195 points, 64 comments) was mixed and technically engaged. Topfi ran an independent test: "In my minimal suite it was cheaper (by 0.72x) but higher latency (283ms vs 369ms p50) than Jev. Results were very comparable across all scenarios I measure, first of these that I have tested that actually justifies its existence as a commercial release." simonw, on the Qwen3.5 base: "I guess the fear of Chinese models is finally subsiding." chris_money202: "not everything needs Opus. Their models are targeting cheap and useful for some common things not expensive and useful for anything."

shouvik12 (dev.to) framed decision models as a new category: "A chat model gives you prose you have to parse. A decision model gives you a fixed set of options with probabilities, which is the shape code already uses: a branch, a threshold, a route." The WindowsForum megathread called it "the strangest product the company has shipped in a year: a model that does not talk" and the start of "the unbundling of the LLM" — generators get bigger while cheap specialist governors handle micro-decisions in agent fleets. Their verdict: "treat Friday's announcement as the opening of an evaluation period, not its conclusion."

Skeptics' checklist, repeated across outlets: all benchmarks are Microsoft's own and not independently reproducible; calibration is unproven on production traffic; Decision-1 doesn't appear on the independent JevBench board (where H2O-Lightning-4B ranks #1, and H2O.ai disputes the latency math used in comparisons). The Decoder: "Jev, the startup that kicked off the decision model trend in mid-September, has likely had a rough few weeks." Markets noticed: MSFT gained ~2.3% on Friday, closing around $535 (~$3.97T market cap) per business press.

What to take away

This is not a Microsoft invention — it's a category entry. TypeSafe's Jev started it mid-September, OpenAI's Decisions API went GA this week (powered by GPT-6 Luna), Cloudflare's Clef launched Oct 1 (see the Oct 4 Morning Feed tip), plus H2O-Lightning-4B, Upstage Solar Decide, and Strands Decider 2B. Microsoft is entering, not creating, the category.

For a platform builder, the actionable pattern is the same one the Clef brief flagged: gate agent actions behind cheap, calibrated decision models instead of expensive LLM calls. Decision-1 at $0.042/M tokens is Microsoft's bid to be the default governor layer for agent fleets built on Foundry. Treat the vendor benchmarks as the opening of an evaluation period — independent testers so far say it's genuinely competitive with Jev, which is more than most entrants can claim.
