Gemini 4 Argon goes global — in Antigravity for Ultra subscribers only

Added Oct 13 · original article

Google's flagship Gemini 4 model is now live in its agentic IDE — but only for top-tier subscribers, while 10,000 scientists get a free six-month pass.

What the post is about

One caveat up front: the post itself could not be read directly, so its exact wording is unconfirmed. What is established is its subject and timing — it landed around midday EDT on October 13, 2026, the same day Google made its flagship Gemini 4 Argon model globally available.

From Google's official announcement (by Koray Kavukcuoglu): Gemini 4 Argon is now globally available via the paid Gemini API and AI Studio — and, key for this account's audience, for Google AI Ultra subscribers inside Google Antigravity (desktop app, CLI, and Android Studio Canary). Pro and Ultra subscribers also get it in the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search; free-tier users do not. Alongside the model, Google opened applications for 10,000 scientists to receive six months free at Antigravity's highest usage tier, onboarded on a rolling basis.

Model economics: 1M unified input-output token limit; introductory API price $2/1M input and $10/1M output, rising to $4/$20 after November 30, 2026. Google's internal scorecard for Argon in Antigravity: agentic code submissions up more than 35%, code rollbacks down nearly a third — vendor claims, no baseline disclosed.

What people are saying

Reuters framed it as a bid to "regain ground against rivals OpenAI and Anthropic after months of delays and a leadership shakeup at its DeepMind lab" (Demis Hassabis stepping aside), and confirmed the Ultra-only Antigravity gating plus the scientists program; early enterprise testers named include Deloitte, LexisNexis, Ambience Healthcare, and Isomorphic Labs.

The most practitioner-useful read came from explainx.ai: Artificial Analysis puts Argon at 53 on the Intelligence Index (tied with GPT-6 Astra, behind Claude Opus 5.5 at 58); Arena.ai ranks it #1 on text but #8 on Code Arena WebDev. Crucially, on Google's own table Argon loses FrontierSWE v2, Terminal-bench 4.0, and Terminal-Bench Science — the advice is to "keep Astra or Opus 5.5 as the control when the API opens" for terminal-agent harnesses, and to budget for the 1M output cap.

The-decoder notes Argon trails Opus 5.5 independently and now sits behind Meta's Muse Wonder Full (launched Oct 12); it also reports secondhand that Google is internally testing a successor codenamed "Carbon" (unverified). Robby Stein (VP of Product, Google Search) announced Argon rolling out in AI Mode for Pro/Ultra — but Search Engine Journal's own check at 12:17 p.m. ET found it absent even on a Pro account, evidence the rollout is gradual. A Hacker News preview thread (1,105 comments) argued the model is gated, "agy is months behind other harnesses," and 3.8 Flash remains the daily driver — with one bright spot: a 15% hallucination rate vs 51% for GPT-6 Astra. Reddit sentiment (Oct 13) is dominated by access confusion, with Pro-vs-Ultra as the fault line.

What to take away

This is a flagship-model rollout into an agentic coding IDE your teams evaluate and adopt — the Ultra-only gating, the token economics ($2/$10 intro pricing stepping up after Nov 30), and the gap between Google's internal productivity claims and independent benchmarks are all directly actionable. The pattern to watch: flagship models are increasingly being used as subscription-tier leverage (Ultra-only inside Antigravity) rather than being broadly available — factor that into platform evaluations.

Note the benchmark discipline from explainx: keep a known control (Astra or Opus 5.5) when evaluating any new model in your own harnesses, and treat vendor #1 claims as the opening of an evaluation period, not its conclusion.
