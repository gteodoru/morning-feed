1Password for Agents: AI agents that sign in without ever seeing your password

Added Oct 22 · original article

1Password's new platform lets personal AI agents sign into your accounts without ever seeing your passwords — the agent asks, you approve on your device, and 1Password types the credential itself.

What the post is about

The trending page is X's summary of the Oct 22, 2026 launch of 1Password for Agents, announced from Toronto at 9:00 AM EDT. The problem it targets: personal AI agents increasingly need to sign into accounts to do real work (bookings, reorders, expenses), and today users either log in themselves or paste a password into a chat window, leaving a copy outside their control.

How it works: when an agent reaches a login screen, it identifies itself to 1Password with a unique digital key and requests a specific credential. The user sees which login the agent wants and why, and approves or denies on their own device. If approved, 1Password fills the credential directly into the site — the agent never receives the password. One-time MFA codes are handled through the same 1Password-managed channel. Access is scoped per session to the specific items approved, with no carryover to the next task.

Availability: included at no extra cost in 1Password Individual and Family plans on 1Password.com (accounts on 1Password.eu and 1Password.ca are not supported). Meta's Muse and SpaceXSI's Grok Bot roll out over the coming days; Anthropic's Claude Duet, OpenAI's ChatGPT Work and dots, and Town's Townie are listed as coming soon. Browserbase, Browser Use, Dana, Instinct, KERNEL, Perplexity, Ramp, Tomo, and Wajo are building integrations. It builds on 1Password for Claude (July 16, 2026), which worked only on Mac inside Chrome. 1Password cited 1.6B+ credentials/secrets in its vaults and 200K+ business customers; X's trending summary put the company at $500M in annual revenue.

What people are saying

The launch post from @1Password (235K views) lists the partner roster. David Singleton (@dps, on the Muse team) posted that they are bringing 1Password directly into Muse, calling it another secure way to let Muse sign in — notable because Muse is one of the named partners. KERNEL (@usekernel) confirmed support: the agent can sign in without ever seeing the password. The Verge's headline frames it the same way: agents can use your passwords without seeing them.

1Password's business chief John Torrey told PCWorld the design principle is that the user is involved in every decision — customers decide which credentials an agent can use, for which task, and for how long, and the agent cannot approve its own access. CEO Aashish Tripathi: the old model of handing over a credential and hoping for the best doesn't work now that agents do real work.

The skeptical takes focus on what happens after sign-in. RR Tech Pulse's read of the FAQ: the credential is protected, but once signed in an agent may see information or take actions in the account — a fast approval is the weak point, because the password is safe but the signed-in session is not. ToolRadar's earlier analysis of the Claude integration noted the reaction split between 'finally' and 'absolutely not,' and that the real question for approvers is where the guarantee ends. PCWorld also noted two absences from the launch roster: Google's Gemini Spark and Microsoft's Scout. Discussion on Blind is running the convenience-vs-risk debate; no substantial Hacker News thread yet.

What to take away

This is the credential-delegation pattern for agents becoming a product category: scoped, per-session, human-approved sign-in where the secret never enters the model's context. For the AI foundation platform work, it is a reference design for how enterprise agents will authenticate to third-party systems — identity asserted by the agent (unique key), authorization granted by the human (device approval), secret injected by a trusted broker (the vault), never exposed to the model. The open questions are the ones the skeptics raise: session-level risk after sign-in, approval fatigue turning per-login prompts into rubber stamps, and roster politics (Gemini Spark and Scout missing at launch). For Gabriel personally: he already uses Muse, which is a named partner — worth watching when the Muse integration ships.
