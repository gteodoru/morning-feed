DeepMind's AlphaGenome Atlas maps all 9 billion possible human DNA mutations

Bloomberg Tech In Depth · Sep 9

Google's AI lab precomputed predictions for every single-letter change in the human genome — a 1-petabyte database, free for researchers.

**What happened:** Google's DeepMind released AlphaGenome Atlas, a free tool that predicts how DNA mutations affect the way genes work. To build it, DeepMind precomputed its AlphaGenome model's predictions for all 9 billion possible single-letter changes in the human genome and packed them into a 1-petabyte database. Scientists can now pull predictions for millions of variants at once instead of repeatedly querying the model, and Atlas also hints at *why* a mutation matters — which biological processes and cell types it may affect.

**Why it matters:** Only 2% of the genome codes for proteins; the remaining 98% was once dismissed as 'junk' DNA. Genetic studies now link non-coding variation to cancer, diabetes, and heart disease — 'It's not junk at all,' as Imperial College's Alessia David put it. Atlas turns a frontier model into research infrastructure.

**The playbook:** DeepMind is running AlphaFold's script again — release powerful scientific tools for free to researchers, charge commercial users (including sister company Isomorphic Labs) a license fee. As DeepMind's Pushmeet Kohli framed it, fundamental breakthroughs feed the commercial side. For an AI foundation platform: build the model, open the tools, monetize the enterprise tier. Watch the licensing terms — they are the product strategy.

Anthropic committed $80B to neoclouds in five days

Chamath Palihapitiya · Sep 8

Anthropic reportedly signed $45B with Nscale and $35B with Lambda — frontier labs are renting GPUs from a new class of cloud built only for AI.

**What happened:** Two little-known cloud companies reportedly received $80 billion in compute contracts from Anthropic within days — a six-year, $45 billion agreement with Nscale and a $35 billion agreement with Lambda. Frontier labs are reaching past AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud to secure the compute they need.

**What neoclouds are:** companies that own GPU capacity and rent access by the hour or under long-term contract — a category that barely existed three years ago and is forecast to approach $400B in revenue by 2031. Their structural advantages: GPU hours 50–70% below hyperscaler list prices, and earlier access to scarce Nvidia GPUs (Nvidia has a strategic incentive to allocate chips to them, expanding market access and reducing concentration risk). AI training changed the physical layer too: rack power jumps from 10–15 kW to 40–250 kW, demanding new networking, electrical systems, and often liquid cooling.

**The network effects:** Nvidia invested $2B each in CoreWeave and Nebius in 2026 — chips plus capital to feed its own distribution channel. CoreWeave's contracted backlog hit $104.2B by end of Q2. The hybrid case: xAI built Colossus 1 to train its own models, but after SpaceX's acquisition it started selling compute to other frontier labs — Anthropic contracted for all of Colossus 1 (300+ MW, 220,000+ Nvidia GPUs), with a roadmap toward one million GPUs. SpaceX is now simultaneously an AI developer and a neocloud.

**The bear case (don't skip it):** neoclouds borrow billions at a premium to buy depreciating hardware, then rent to customers who are themselves building capacity and custom chips. Chamath's 88-page Deep Dive frames the core question as the gap between contracted growth and economic durability — and argues inference, not training, is the workload that actually sustains the model.
