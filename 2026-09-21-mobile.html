John Ternus's "intelligent personal hub": the iPhone stays the center of Apple's AI era

Bloomberg Power On (Mark Gurman) · Sep 21

Gurman details the long-awaited home hub — facial recognition without a depth sensor, a Siri AI app that searches across your devices — and the Fitness+ cuts that reveal Apple's real priorities.

When John Ternus opened his first keynote as Apple's CEO, he asked viewers to imagine designing an ideal AI device from scratch: always-on connectivity, a decent display, top-tier cameras and mics, great battery, easy to use, interoperable. The punchline was that the phone-centric world lasts "longer than many people think" — AR glasses replacing the iPhone are still many years away, and the AI-powered iPhone remains the center of everything.

The home piece of that thesis is the long-awaited hub, codenamed J490, now being tested widely in employees' homes. It's a roughly square display in stand or wall-mount versions — Gurman compares it to a HomePod mini chopped in half — running a mashup of iOS, tvOS and watchOS. Its headline trick: facial recognition through the front camera that identifies who's looking and personalizes the screen — apps, notes, calendar, news, music, podcasts — without a Face ID-level depth sensor. Gurman notes this technique could eventually bring facial recognition to the iPhone Duo (currently Touch ID) even without the physical space, though unlocking-level security remains the open question. A Siri AI app would let users search across devices and accounts — "play the podcast someone sent me a while ago," find a photo, act on Notes/Reminders/Calendar — and run more complex in-app tasks.

This is Steve Jobs's old three-settings doctrine (home, mobile, car) under a new CEO: iPhone anchors mobile, the hub plus a new HomePod mini and a TV set-top box with Siri AI claim the household.

The tell on where the money moves: Apple cut a handful of Fitness+ staff last week — mostly the audio side behind Time to Walk and Time to Run — and inside the org it's read as the start of deeper cost-cutting. Gurman's bet: Fitness+ eventually folds into the revamped Health app (both now sit in the same org under Eddy Cue). Also in the newsletter: the iPhone Duo Pencil accessory ran into technical problems before mass production, so Apple pivoted to supporting iPad Pencils via software — "fairly late in 2026" — and Apple Pay's India launch is imminent.

SoftBank launches a record $11B+ junk-bond sale to keep funding OpenAI

Bloomberg Markets Daily (via Seoul Economic Daily) · Sep 21

Proceeds cover the $10B third OpenAI tranche due October 1 — with borrowing costs rising and Altman ruling out an IPO this year, Son's $65B bet gets more leveraged by the week.

SoftBank plans to issue $10 billion in dollar-denominated junk bonds plus €1 billion in euro notes, in what would be among the largest speculative-grade bond sales ever by a single corporate issuer. The proceeds fund the third $10 billion tranche of its follow-on investment in OpenAI, due October 1, and replace a $10 billion bridge loan. Pricing is set for September 24, settling September 29, with Citi and JPMorgan as lead bookrunners.

The numbers around Son's bet: nearly $65 billion committed to OpenAI in total, an estimated ~13% stake, $40 billion in bridge financing arranged earlier this year, and an Arm-backed margin loan limit raised from $5 billion to $25 billion. Borrowing is getting expensive: the yield on SoftBank's 2031 dollar bonds rose to 8.2% this month from 6.7% in January, and the cost of insuring against default hit a three-year high. As Bloomberg notes, expectations for greater liquidity in the stake are being shaken by the industry's safety debate and by Sam Altman saying an IPO won't happen this year. One man's junk bond is another man's AI conviction trade — and Son is now the market's most leveraged expression of it.

Profound raises $180M at $1.8B to build the "AI Marketer"

Bloomberg Tech In Depth; Profound · Sep 21

Sequoia and Kleiner Perkins co-lead a $180M Series D at $1.8B — the AEO category is now a budget line, with incumbents and a pack of startups fighting over it.

Profound, a New York startup founded in 2024, raised $180 million in Series D at a $1.8 billion post-money valuation, co-led by Sequoia Capital and Kleiner Perkins, with Lightspeed, Khosla, Saga, Evantic and South Park Commons participating. It arrives less than seven months after a $96 million Series C at $1 billion — and total funding now tops $335 million.

The category is answer-engine optimization (AEO): managing how a brand appears when consumers ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity instead of typing into Google. Profound started as analytics for brand visibility inside AI answers and is now shipping an "AI Marketer" — an agent orchestrator that researches, writes and reports alongside marketing teams, plus a Context Manager that continuously synthesizes brand files and working styles, and an Ads Studio for campaigns across OpenAI, Google and Meta. The company says it serves 1,000+ enterprise brands including Comcast, Walmart, Estée Lauder and over a third of the Fortune 100; TechCrunch reports revenue tripled in six months (company-reported).

Why this matters beyond one round: incumbents are adapting rather than ceding the budget line — Semrush shipped an AI Visibility Toolkit, Ahrefs shipped Brand Radar — while a pack of AEO natives (Peec AI, Otterly.AI, Scrunch AI, Bluefish AI, Rankability, AthenaHQ) chases the same spend. Profound's wager is the classic vertical-software one: a purpose-built platform beats an SEO suite with AI features grafted on. For anyone driving AI adoption inside an org, AEO is the concrete shape of "the chatbot ate search" — it changes what marketing teams measure, and who they hire.

Nscale's $3B IPO bid: the neocloud stress test

Bloomberg Tech In Depth; Temperature 2 · Sep 21

Nscale's S-1 shows a $1.02B loss on $140.6M of revenue — against a $51B contracted backlog. With Crusoe raising $4B in parallel, the neocloud trade moves from funding rounds to refinancing rounds.

The London-based AI data-center builder filed its Form S-1 on September 18 and is seeking as much as $3 billion in a US IPO (ticker NSCL), with Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley running the deal — per Bloomberg. The S-1 makes the economics legible: a net loss of $1.02 billion on revenue of just $140.6 million for the six months ended June 30 (against $368.9 million of losses on $10.4 million a year earlier). Revenue grew ~13x; losses grew ~2.8x.

Against that stands a $51 billion contracted-revenue backlog, ~289,000 active and contracted GPUs (about 194,000 of them Nvidia's Vera Rubin), and partnerships with Nvidia — which holds 5%+ via warrants — Microsoft and Anthropic. Founded in 2024 as a spinout of crypto miner Arkon Energy, Nscale raised a $1.1 billion Series B in late 2025 and a $2 billion Series C in March at $14.6 billion with Nvidia, Dell and Nokia participating. Total financing so far: $3.7 billion in equity, over $5 billion in debt.

Meanwhile Crusoe's CEO Chase Lochmiller told Bloomberg Tech the cloud provider will use $4 billion in fresh funding for new AI data centers. The neocloud trade is no longer a raise story; it's a ladder of refinancings, each rung bigger than the last. The IPO is the stress test: public markets get to price a construction project, not a cash engine.

AI boosters are misreading the public

Bloomberg Technology (Austin Carr); Bloomberg Law; Bloomberg Markets Daily · Sep 21

Trump wants an "AI Force" and a new AI czar and calls safety fears a hoax — while polls show voters anxious about data centers and the market asks what a slowdown would cost.

Austin Carr's cover piece in Bloomberg's Tech In Depth names the gap: calls by Trump and the industry's loudest boosters to press forward are clashing with voter sentiment — and even with figures inside the president's own party.

The White House position is now maximal acceleration. During a stage appearance on the All-In podcast, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang phoned Trump, who dismissed societal concerns about AI as a "conspiracy" and told the crowd nobody should worry about data centers: they bring "incredible prosperity." On Truth Social, Trump announced an "AI Force" modeled on Space Force and a new AI czar ("High IQ" applicants only), writing: "We will not in any way hinder or stifle the Growth of this incredible Industry." He called AI-safety concern a "SICK conspiracy" that only China is happy about, and said the only guardrail AI needs is "a STRONG AND SMART (High IQ!) PRESIDENT."

This landed in the middle of the month's safety cascade: Anthropic researchers casually suggested a sizable chance AI kills all humans within a decade; Dario Amodei's essay called on frontier labs to pace model improvements and coordinate with governments; OpenAI said it's ramping up safety measures. Musk backed Amodei's pacing case, then walked it back the same day to "some oversight." Vance: "If you're building Frankenstein, stop." Huang and ex-AI-czar David Sacks rejected an industrywide slowdown, arguing companies should innovate full speed while managing risks individually. Geoffrey Hinton reportedly told a closed-door Senate briefing that Congress has about a year to regulate.

The polls: a Times/Siena poll found less than 1% of likely voters cite AI as the top issue for November's midterms — but broad anxiety about the infrastructure. Pew's June report: a majority of US adults expect AI to mean fewer jobs over the next 20 years; only ~7% of right-leaning respondents think AI creates jobs. In Wisconsin, opposition to data centers rose double-digits across all parties over the past year. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent — now shaping the administration's AI-safety stance — complained tech companies have done a "horrendous job of explaining themselves to the American people."

And the market stakes of the "tap the brakes" conversation, per Markets Daily: AI-related spending accounts for about half of US GDP growth by some estimates, and almost $33 trillion in market value has been added to the S&P 500 since late 2022 — the vast majority from companies whose futures are tethered to AI. Patriarch's Eric Schiffer: "One good earnings season doesn't dismiss the long-term disruptive potential... there won't be a time out for disruption."

States are moving where Washington won't: Newsom created a working group to design AI safety measures — "he wants a kill switch" — and Anthropic will "partner" with Accenture, embedding evaluators to assess new models' safety.

US and China propose an "AI dialogue" ahead of this week's Trump–Xi summit

Bloomberg Markets Daily; CNN · Sep 21

Bessent called eight hours with He Lifeng "very successful": a notification mechanism for national-security-level AI incidents is on the table ahead of the leaders' summit.

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng held around eight hours of talks at JPMorgan's New York headquarters, with US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer also at the table. Bessent called it "a very successful engagement," and the two sides agreed to meet again ahead of the leaders' summit in Washington this week.

The concrete item: the US proposed a "US-China AI dialogue" with a notification mechanism for potential AI "incidents" that rise to national-security level — "moving from opaque to more transparency between the number one and the number two AI powers in the world," in Bessent's words. Greer said export controls on sophisticated AI chips were not on the agenda. Xinhua's readout acknowledged an AI dialogue without detail, calling the overall talks "candid, in-depth and constructive." Trade remains the other clock ticking: the current US-China tariff truce expires November 10.
